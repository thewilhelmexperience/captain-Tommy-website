# Captain Tommy Character Bible

Date: 2026-04-16
Project: `captain-tommy-website`
Purpose: Keep future site writing cohesive, personal, and true to Tommy's voice as more stories, chapters, and travel notes get added.

## Who Tommy is

Tommy is calm, funny, unflappable, observant, and highly capable.

He is not trying to seem unique for effect. He is genuinely an individual, and the site should let that show without forcing it into a brand-performance box.

He has a steady presence when things are going wrong. People under his care or command should feel secure because he tends to process what is actually happening, identify immediate steps, and improve the situation instead of letting emotion take over.

He also has a sharp sense of humor. There is room for wit, but it should feel natural, dry, and human, not corny or constantly on display.

- Responsible with serious assets and serious situations
- Deeply drawn to movement, change, and variety

## How Tommy thinks about the life

Tommy is drawn to the constant change in maritime life.

He loves that each harbor can feel like its own country, with its own culture, pace, and history. He notices the human side of places, not just the postcard side. Islands, cities, coastlines, and lake towns all matter because of the people in them and the way life is shaped there.

He feels most at home in the islands and South Florida rhythm, but he is adaptable almost anywhere. He can make inroads quickly in unfamiliar places and build the right local relationships. He pays attention to local character and enjoys becoming part of the fabric wherever he is.

He is fascinated by the human condition across places. What stays with him is often not just scenery, but the way people live, solve problems, and relate to each other.

## How Tommy thinks about boats

Driving the boat is the easy part.

The real work is responsibility: safety of the people aboard, preservation of a serious asset, crew cohesion, system knowledge, maintenance standards, preparation, and owner experience.

Tommy does not approach a new boat with blind trust. He has seen too many situations where a vessel was presented as well-kept or organized when it was not. But he also does not approach it with pure cynicism. He expects the human element to be imperfect and begins in absorb mode:

- learn the boat
- learn the crew
- learn the owner
- understand what the boat actually has to give
- find the gaps
- organize the whole thing into a cohesive program

He enjoys bringing order to chaos and getting a vessel into true travel-ready shape.

## What "trusting the vessel" means

Trust is layered. It is not just whether the engines turn over.

To trust a vessel means understanding:

- her honest history, when available
- the condition of safety, emergency, and lifesaving gear
- the competence and cohesion of the crew
- the quality of maintenance and repairs
- the state of systems and paperwork
- what the vessel is actually capable of in real conditions

Tommy needs to know what a boat has to give before he can truly trust her.

## What makes a chapter memorable

Each boat chapter has its own shape.

A chapter can become memorable because of:

- the condition of the vessel when he arrived
- the owners and how they shaped the experience
- the crew dynamic
- the waters traveled
- the problems solved
- how much the vessel changed under his care
- the people and places tied to that period

Tommy especially values the feeling of taking a boat from disorder, uncertainty, or deferred maintenance and getting her back to a place of reliability and routine.

## What the site should feel like

The site should feel:

- personal
- human
- capable
- lived in
- adventure-adjacent without showing off
- captain-like without being stiff
- reflective without becoming poetic fluff

It should feel like a place where:

- friends and family can keep up with the journey
- employers can understand the person behind the CV
- Tommy can chronicle the voyage of his life before details fade with time

## What the site is not

Do not let the writing drift into:

- corporate language
- macho posturing
- influencer travel-blog energy
- fake luxury-yacht marketing
- self-important sea-philosopher voice
- bragging or false grandeur
- overproduced "personal brand" speak

If the choice is between polished and honest, choose honest.
If the choice is between dramatic and credible, choose credible.
If the choice is between slick and human, choose human.

## Copy style guidance

Writing should sound like someone competent and observant, not someone auditioning to be a lifestyle icon.

Preferred qualities:

- direct but warm
- specific when details are available
- comfortable with understatement
- captain-like in authority
- occasionally wry
- grounded in real responsibility
- attentive to people, places, and change over time

Avoid:

- excessive metaphors
- anything that sounds like tourism marketing
- generic motivational language
- overexplaining obvious competence
- trying too hard to sound legendary

## Content shaping rules for future additions

When turning Tommy's raw notes into site copy:

1. Preserve the human observation, not just the event.
2. Let places have character, not just names.
3. Let competence show through decisions and outcomes, not chest-thumping.
4. Keep responsibility visible.
5. Favor lived details over polished slogans.
6. When in doubt, sound more like a real captain and less like a campaign.

### Additional voice truths seen in Tommy's own writing

- He naturally alternates between hard operational detail and deadpan humor.
- He often makes a serious point by first showing the mess in plain language instead of dressing it up.
- He does not romanticize dirty or difficult work, but he does enjoy the absurdity when it is real.
- His voice gets stronger when specific failures, systems, places, or human behavior are named plainly.
- He likes competence to show through action, improvisation, and follow-through, not through self-congratulation.
- When a fragment is expanded, keep the blunt lived specificity. Do not sand it down into polished travel writing.
- Memory fragments can stay short and still be valuable if they carry a real operational or emotional transition.
- He often frames impossible or unreasonable situations with dry undercutting language instead of dramatic emphasis. Phrases like "that was enough to turn the day from impossible into merely stupid" or titles like "The Sound System Worked Great" reflect how he naturally lets irony do the work.
- He tends to trust the reader to understand competence from what got handled, not from any explicit claim that he handled it well.
- He is especially strong when writing from contrast: calm water on a lake that is usually not calm, a place that reveals itself after the wrong people leave, a beautiful boat that is mechanically sick, a quiet anchorage that feels older than the modern world around it.
- He notices when a place stops being scenery and becomes socially or emotionally real. That transition is a recurring center of gravity in his memories and stories.
- He often treats operational madness as normal until the accumulating details make the absurdity obvious. Preserve that pacing instead of front-loading judgment.
- He respects seasonal mood and regional psychology, especially in places he knows deeply. Early spring on the Great Lakes, island rhythm in the Bahamas, and the character of specific ports should be treated as real content, not decorative atmosphere.
- He is good at showing how ownership, management, crew quality, and maintenance culture shape a boat chapter before anyone says so directly.
- He often uses one vivid, slightly funny, or quietly incriminating detail to summarize a whole situation. Those details should be protected in rewrites.

### Expansion guardrails for future fragments

If Tommy gives only a fragment:
- preserve the original signal or point of the memory
- expand around it without overwriting the tone
- avoid turning a concise remembered truth into a theatrical monologue
- keep the prose grounded in actual conditions, duties, systems, people, and place changes
- allow one sharp funny line when it is earned
- preserve contrast structures when they are present, because Tommy often uses "what should have been happening" versus "what was actually happening" as the engine of the piece
- let place character and human behavior carry meaning before adding editorial explanation
- when a boat or operation is unhealthy, show the symptoms first and let the diagnosis emerge from them
- if the best version of a piece depends on one incriminating, funny, or deeply specific detail, do not replace it with smoother summary
