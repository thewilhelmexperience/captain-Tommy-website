# Written Content Ingestion Directive

Date: 2026-04-21
Project: `captain-tommy-website-publish`
Purpose: Define how raw Tommy writing becomes structured site content without losing truth, voice, or momentum.

## Why this exists

The site becomes real when Tommy can send rough material from life as it is being lived and the system can reliably turn that material into publishable content.

If intake is weak, every post becomes a manual rescue.
If intake is strong, the site grows naturally from reality.

This directive exists to keep intake:
- fast enough to use regularly
- structured enough to organize
- faithful enough to sound like Tommy
- flexible enough to handle both polished writing and rough fragments

## The governing rule

Every new piece of Tommy writing is two things at once:
1. content input
2. voice evidence

Before expanding or reshaping raw material, consult `CHARACTER_BIBLE.md`.
If new material reveals a repeatable voice pattern, update `CHARACTER_BIBLE.md`.

## Accepted input forms

Tommy may send any of the following:
- a full story
- a dated log or passage account
- a short note or observation
- a fragmented memory
- a voicey paragraph with no clear bucket yet
- a blunt operational anecdote
- a place memory
- a people-centered memory
- a rough pile of details that still needs shape

The system should not reject useful material just because it arrives rough.
Fragments are valid source material.

## Primary content buckets

### 1. Logs
Use for:
- dated passages
- crossings
- deliveries
- timeline events
- movement from one place to another
- operational sequences with a clear when/where

Logs should preserve:
- time and movement
- conditions and decisions
- the practical reality of the run
- enough texture to feel lived, not just recorded

### 2. Stories
Use for:
- bigger narrative pieces
- memorable incidents
- boat chapters
- people-rich scenes
- events with shape, tension, absurdity, or payoff

Stories should preserve:
- the scene
- the people
- the problem or energy in motion
- Tommy's lived perspective
- the earned humor, if present

### 3. Notes
Use for:
- short truths
- recurring rules
- practical observations
- route or seamanship lessons
- place notes
- compact pieces that do not need full story treatment

Notes should preserve:
- clarity
- usefulness
- bluntness when earned
- compact human truth

### 4. Memories
Use for:
- fragments that carry emotional or transitional weight
- short place memories
- a sharp remembered image or realization
- moments that matter more for what they reveal than for chronology

Memories should preserve:
- the original signal
- specificity
- compression when it helps
- emotional truth without melodrama

## Bucket selection rule

Do not force the first draft into a perfect category too early.

Ask:
- Is this mainly about movement through time and place? -> log
- Is this mainly about a scene, incident, or narrative arc? -> story
- Is this mainly a compact truth, lesson, or observation? -> note
- Is this mainly a remembered moment, image, or personal turn? -> memory

If a piece could fit more than one bucket, choose the form that best preserves its natural energy.

## Intake workflow

### Step 1: Receive raw material
Capture what Tommy actually sent.
Do not overwrite the signal too early.

### Step 2: Identify the dominant shape
Decide whether it wants to be a log, story, note, or memory.
If unclear, keep it provisional until the shape reveals itself.

### Step 3: Preserve the anchor truths
Before rewriting, identify:
- what happened
- where it happened
- who mattered
- what made it worth remembering
- what tone is already present
- what specific lived details must survive

### Step 4: Expand carefully
When expanding:
- preserve Tommy's operational phrasing
- keep the dry understatement
- let competence show through action
- name real places, systems, failures, duties, and people when available
- keep the prose grounded in real conditions
- avoid sanding rough truths into generic polished copy

### Step 5: Classify and structure
Once the piece has shape, assign it to the right bucket and prepare metadata as needed.

### Step 6: Learn from the piece
If the raw material reveals a recurring voice pattern, update `CHARACTER_BIBLE.md`.
The model should improve as real input accumulates.

## Expansion rules by input type

### If Tommy sends a strong full draft
- preserve structure unless it is clearly broken
- tighten lightly rather than rewriting heavily
- treat the prose as primary voice evidence

### If Tommy sends a rough but detailed paragraph
- keep the best lines
- organize around the natural center of gravity
- do not erase the bluntness that gave it life

### If Tommy sends bullet points or compressed details
- infer structure carefully
- do not invent drama
- only add connective tissue needed for clarity and flow

### If Tommy sends a memory fragment
- treat it as legitimate content, not as a lesser draft
- preserve the remembered point or image
- expand only enough to make it land
- do not turn a concise truth into a theatrical essay

## What must survive the rewrite

Whenever possible, preserve:
- place character
- human behavior
- operational detail
- responsibility
- absurdity, when real
- small lived specifics
- Tommy's calm authority
- Tommy's dry humor

## What must not happen

Do not let ingestion drift into:
- tourism copy
- influencer travel tone
- recruiter-flattened life summary
- macho sea mythology
- fake literary gravitas
- overexplained competence
- assistant-sounding glue prose
- decorative detail that replaces real detail

## Editing standard

A successful pass should feel like:
- Tommy, but clearer
- Tommy, but better shaped
- Tommy, but still unmistakably Tommy

A failed pass feels like:
- an assistant performing tastefulness
- generic nice writing with maritime nouns
- a cleaned-up version that lost the original voltage

## Interview and prompting strategy

When Tommy is not sure what to write next, prompts should aim at memory retrieval, not homework.

Good prompting tends to ask for:
- the first moment something felt wrong
- the dirtiest or funniest real detail
- a boat that changed under his care
- a place that surprised him
- a crew or owner dynamic that defined a chapter
- a crossing decision he still remembers clearly
- a small rule he follows because he learned it the hard way
- a moment where competence mattered quietly
- a place that only makes sense if you have really been there
- a memory that still has a smell, texture, or sound attached to it

Prompt toward underfilled buckets instead of repeating the same lane.

## Current operating principle

The goal is not to manufacture content.
The goal is to help real Tommy material arrive in the right shape, in the right voice, with as little friction as possible.

When in doubt:
- choose truth over polish
- choose specificity over generality
- choose lived detail over summary
- choose Tommy's voice over assistant cleverness
