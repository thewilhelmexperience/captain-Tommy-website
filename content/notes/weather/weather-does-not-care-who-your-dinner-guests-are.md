Sometimes part of the job is standing up to the boss.

There is a reason they need you, and one of those reasons is that somebody on board has to care more about reality than about the owner’s preferred outcome. Weather is one of the clearest examples.

Weather windows are not a formality. They are not a suggestion, and they are not something you get to negotiate with because the schedule became inconvenient. If a major system is moving through, if the conditions are unsafe, or if the risk has crossed the line from manageable to foolish, then that is the answer. Not the beginning of a debate.

Owners can try every tactic in the book when they do not like that answer. Schedule pressure, guest pressure, sunk-cost pressure, irritation, status, guilt, confidence, the belief that money should be able to bend circumstances, all of it shows up sooner or later. None of it changes the weather.

Mother Nature does not care that dinner guests are only in town for one more night. She does not care what was promised, what was planned, or how badly somebody wants the evening to go a certain way. She is not involved in the negotiation.

And neither should you be, once the answer is clear.

If you are the person responsible for the boat and the people aboard, then sometimes your job is to disappoint somebody on purpose. Better that than pretending conditions are acceptable just because the boss is not in the mood to hear otherwise.

We are not going out in a named storm.
