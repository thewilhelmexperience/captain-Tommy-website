In July 2025 I was on *Lady Victory*, tied up at Chelsea Piers in Manhattan. The owners had been traveling most of June, and we were not supposed to be in the Hamptons yet. That was an August plan. No dockage had been arranged, no Fourth of July setup existed, and under normal circumstances none of this would have been attempted on a holiday weekend.

Then on July 3rd I got the call asking if we could move the boat to Sag Harbor so they could watch the fireworks.

Under ideal conditions it is about a seven-hour run. This was not ideal. The Fourth landed on a Friday, which meant peak summer weekend, prime destination, and the sort of availability problem that usually gets solved long before the day before. I started calling around anyway. Most of my contacts in Sag Harbor laughed, which was a reasonable reaction, but eventually somebody found me a mooring ball.

That was enough to turn the day from impossible into merely stupid.

The crew's first reaction was basically, no chance this can play. By the end of the day it had turned into, I think we might actually pull this off.

And to their credit, they did.

By the time we got to Sag Harbor, *Lady Victory* was standing tall on the mooring because the crew had pulled out all the stops. We had no chef, so everything had to be picked up in town during setup. Our stew grabbed Fourth of July decorations in Manhattan before we left, and by that night there were flags strung up everywhere and the interior actually looked better than the whole operation had any right to. The boss did not seem to notice, but it looked good.

Guest transfers were exactly the kind of mess you would expect from a last-minute holiday plan involving wealthy people and a waterfront town. Everyone seemed to arrive at once and expected to be shuttled out immediately. Several went to the wrong dock despite having perfectly good directions, which they apparently found too burdensome to follow. At one point the stew ran back into town on the shuttle boat for provisions, then got trapped trying to return because the public shuttle dock had turned into a complete mob scene.

There were plenty of small hiccups through setup, but by fireworks time we had done it. We hosted the evening, got everyone where they were supposed to be, and gave the owners their Sag Harbor Fourth of July as if this had all been sensibly arranged in advance.

For us, it was a grind. We were exhausted. But there was one unexpected reward: that night on the mooring was the first time since leaving Florida that we were not being constantly rocked by ferry wake. It was a short night, but all of us woke up saying it was the first good sleep we had had in weeks.

The next morning we came off the mooring, fueled at Sag Harbor Yacht Club, and ran all the way back to Chelsea Piers. By 6 p.m. we were back in Manhattan, like none of this had been an unreasonable request in the first place.

It was a ridiculous twenty-four hours, most of it was inconvenient for everyone involved except the guests, and by the end of it we were exhausted. But we pulled it off, got a decent night of sleep for the first time since leaving Florida, and made it back to New York feeling like we had gotten away with something.
