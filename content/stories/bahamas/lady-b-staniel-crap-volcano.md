On Lady B in the Bahamas, it was the boss's birthday trip with fourteen women aboard, plus the deckhand and me as the only two males. On arrival to Staniel we discovered that the plumber at Rolly, who had serviced the MSD, hooked up a Y valve wrong. So not only was the black water tank at capacity and close to overflow, the main waste transfer pump tried to cycle against a hose that dead ended into the closed side of the improperly installed valve. The result was a pump that destroyed itself and a full waste tank with sixteen guests aboard.

After an all hands announcement telling everyone the heads were not to be flushed and to use the marina facilities, apparently a couple of the women thought this didn't apply to them and they used the toilets anyway, causing a geyser in the master stateroom through the bidet, which also had a check valve improperly installed.

I found a pump in Fort Lauderdale, had a friend bring it to FXE and put it on the next Makers Air flight to Staniel Cay, jumped through all the Bahamian government hoops to get an import license, received the replacement pump that afternoon, and installed it in the in-flight jet engine repair method, which required me to participate in an enclosed space with an active crap volcano flowing. I also had to rework the Y valve with full waste pipes.

I didn't smell pretty, but the system was repaired and functional and we didn't have to end the trip two weeks into the three month plan. The repair held, and I eventually smelled better. The epitome of the glamorous life of crewing on a yacht in paradise.
