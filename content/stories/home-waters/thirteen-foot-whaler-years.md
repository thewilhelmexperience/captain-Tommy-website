This is a seed example story entry, meant to show how the archive can hold memory and narrative, not just dated movement.

The thirteen-foot Whaler years belong to the beginning, not because they were small, but because they were foundational. A boat like that is remembered differently when it is not a toy or a novelty, but a real way of learning water, distance, weather, and confidence. That is the kind of material that should live under a chapter like Home Waters Runs, even if it does not behave like a single-day log.

A story entry like this gives the system room for memory, reflection, and continuity. It can connect to South Florida places, to a broader life arc, and to representative images, while still remaining distinct from a dated passage log or a short operational note.

This is also where the archive starts to become more than a photo browser. The image may help trigger the memory, but the story gives the picture meaning, context, and emotional weight.
