One of the most peaceful memories I carried away from that trip came from doing something that was technically work.

We were on the ball at Shroud Cay, and the owners wanted to hear me walking the deck every hour through the night. So I kept making my rounds, again and again, while the boat sat alone in the mooring field.

That should have made it feel like a duty.

Instead, it became one of the best parts of the trip.

Every time I came out, my buddy Barry, a barracuda who hunted the edges of the underwater lights, was there in the glow like he had the watch too. I started expecting him. The night had that kind of rhythm to it, where even something a little sharp-toothed starts to feel like part of the company.

But what really made it stay with me was the quiet.

No ambient light. No background noise. No endless shore glow from roads, houses, marinas, or condos. Just the boat, the water, the stars, and whatever else was moving around out there in the dark. The sky was so clear it almost felt unreasonable, like there was more of it than you were supposed to be able to see at once.

And in that kind of quiet, it is hard not to feel connected to the people who moved through places like that hundreds of years ago, before everything got paved over and built up and lit to death. Out there, for a little while, the world feels older again.

Nighttime at Shroud Cay, when you are the only boat in the mooring field, is one of the most peaceful places I have ever been.
