We departed Chelsea Piers in New York on the evening of October 7, 2025 aboard *Lady Victory*, starting south on the seasonal delivery run out of the city. The original destination was the Virginia Beach Fishing Center.

By the time we left, we were already a few days behind schedule. The boat had mechanically protested getting underway in the first place, and the weather window was starting to narrow. Departure from New York Harbor was cold but calm, and for most of the overnight run the trip was fairly uneventful.

As morning came on, the weather started to shift.

We were in contact with Virginia Beach Fishing Center as we approached. That inlet can be unpredictable even on a good day, and they were already reporting shoaling. Local traffic moving through was still giving usable guidance on where the safe water was, and based on those reports we were not especially worried about our 5.5-foot draft. At that stage, getting in still looked possible.

But the closer we got, the less attractive that plan became.

Near the mouth of the Chesapeake, with Virginia Beach only another hour to hour and a half away, the seas were continuing to build and the forecast for the next few days was looking worse, not better. Even if we got in, there was a good chance we would be stuck there for several days. And once the weather passed, there was no telling what the shoaling at the inlet would look like afterward. We were close enough to make it work, but no longer close enough to justify it.

So we changed the plan and diverted to Tidewater Yacht Marina in Portsmouth instead.

It was the safer option, the more stable option, and it also set us up well for the next day. With weather shutting down the outside route anyway, Portsmouth positioned us to continue south on the ICW instead of gambling on a sketchy arrival and then sitting around trapped behind worsening conditions.

We did not get tied up until around midnight, so it made for a long day. But it was still the right call. Better to end tired in the right place than spend the night congratulating yourself for forcing the original plan.

That run stands out because there was no dramatic emergency, just the steady narrowing of a bad option until the smarter one became obvious. Those are the decisions that keep trips moving.
