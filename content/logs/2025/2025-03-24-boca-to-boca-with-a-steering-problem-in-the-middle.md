We departed The Boca Raton on March 24, 2025 aboard *Lady Victory* for what was supposed to be a simple owner cruise, a pleasant run out of the home slip, down toward Fort Lauderdale, and back. The plan was easy enough: ICW to Hillsboro Inlet, out to the ocean, back in through Port Everglades, then a relaxed ride north on the ICW to Boca. Conditions were good, around 80 degrees with light SSE wind, and nothing about the day suggested it was going to become particularly educational.

For the most part, it did not.

The owners had friends aboard and were just out for a boat ride. While we were offshore between Pompano and Fort Lauderdale, I handed the wheel to the mate so I could do a quick engine room check and step away for a minute. When I came back up, he showed me that the rudder indicator was no longer responding to wheel input. The boat was still tracking straight and we were only making about five to six knots, but it was immediately clear that something in the steering system was no longer behaving normally.

I went back down to the engine room and found the reservoir for the power steering pump empty.

I topped it off, repressurized the system, and confirmed over the radio with the mate that steering response had returned. That got us moving normally again, and we continued into Port Everglades. While we were waiting on the Las Olas bridge, I went back down and found the reservoir about halfway drained again. So from there on out, the trip became a quiet exercise in keeping the system alive long enough to get home cleanly. I kept adding fluid, kept checking levels, and kept us moving.

From the guest side, it was still just a nice day on the boat.

That was the whole point.

We completed the trip and made it back to the slip without the owners or their guests ever really knowing there had been an issue. The main challenge, other than the actual steering problem, was trying to keep my uniform looking vaguely presentable while repeatedly disappearing to deal with power steering fluid.

Once we were back, I told the boss what had happened and went straight into the lazarette to find the missing fluid. It was not hard to locate. Most of it was in the bilge, and the source turned out to be a steering hose that had very nearly ruptured completely. That afternoon I had new steering lines made, and the mate and I replaced them.

What stays with me about that day is that from the outside it looked like nothing had happened. Sometimes that is what a successful day actually looks like. Not heroics, not drama, just finding the problem, managing it calmly, and getting everybody home before the issue gets a chance to introduce itself properly.
