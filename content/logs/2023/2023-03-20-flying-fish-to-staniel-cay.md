We departed Flying Fish Marina in Clarence Town, Long Island, on March 20, 2023 aboard *Lady B*, an 85-foot Pacific Mariner, bound for Staniel Cay.

This was the last long passage with the owner and guests still aboard at the tail end of a months-long Bahamas trip. Weather had already delayed our departure by a couple of days, which killed the original plan of working our way back north by shorter island-hopping legs. The guests had departure flights out of Nassau coming up, so we needed to cover a big section of the southern Exumas in one shot and get them back into the neighborhood for the final few days of the trip.

It turned into exactly the kind of day you hope for when the schedule has started to tighten.

Conditions on departure were 76.8°F with light SSE winds around 9 knots. By arrival they had shifted slightly to about 10 knots from the SSW, temperature 74.7°F, with calm seas all day. The gentle wind stayed at our back, the boat ran well, and the guests were actually able to enjoy the day instead of spending it stressed about timing or beat up by weather.

We saw whales on departure, then settled into a smooth run up the sound. By the end of the day we had covered 113 nautical miles in just under nine hours.

The only wrinkle came at Staniel. When we got to the cut, I called Patrice on the radio and found out our slip at the marina was not cleared out yet. She asked if we could give it another hour before coming in.

So we turned around and improvised.

The guests rigged some lines and we went trolling with an 85-foot motoryacht, which is not the most dignified look but got the job done. We ended up landing a four-foot barracuda, which was also the day I learned that our deckhand’s fishing skills were mostly fictional. Fortunately the chef was a real fisherman and stepped in, handled the whole thing cleanly, got the trophy photos, and saw to it that the fish was safely released.

That killed just enough time for our slip to open up. We got into the marina, got everything unstowed, and had the boat settled in time for dinner.

What I remember most about that day is how pleasant it was in spite of the schedule pressure hanging behind it. No weather, no drama, no guest anxiety, just a smooth run, a little improvisation at the end, and the feeling that we had gotten back on schedule without anybody having to suffer for it. After a long trip, that kind of day stands out.
