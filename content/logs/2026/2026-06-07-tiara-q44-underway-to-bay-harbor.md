Left the dock on the Tiara Q44 at 6:30 this morning and pointed her north for Bay Harbor. It was about sixty degrees, light east wind, and a clear sky, which is about as clean a start as you can ask for on a delivery day.

The first part of the run had that rare easy feel Lake Michigan gives you once in a while. North of Muskegon and up toward White Lake it stayed calm and clean, aside from the usual Sunday-morning fishermen parked in exactly the spots that make you adjust your line whether you wanted to or not.

I passed Big Red on the way out, which means I took the mandatory picture. That is not really optional at this point. Some landmarks get grandfathered into habit.

Gunther checked the weather up the route while I was moving north and the forecast held exactly where you want it: no active advisories, light north and northeast wind, and nothing ugly building. My reaction at the time was simple enough: I'll take that from Lake Michigan, because you never know what it's going to give you.

By the time I was up around Big Sable the air had cooled off a little, but the lake was still behaving. There had been some earlier fog concerns farther up the line, but it burned off before I got there, which kept the day in the category every delivery run hopes for and not many actually get.

The only stretch that tightened up much was around Manitou Passage. Passing Sleeping Bear Dunes, the water locked up into about a one-foot chop, just enough to put a little vibration into the ride without turning it into a problem. After that it settled back down again and gave me a pretty smooth finish into Bay Harbor.

Docked at 3:19 in Bay Harbor after a full clean northbound day, the sort of run that reminds you how good a delivery can feel when the boat is moving well, the weather stays honest, and the lake decides not to make you earn every mile the hard way.
