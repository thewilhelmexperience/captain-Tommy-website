We came off the dock in Atlantic City at 7:30 this morning and headed straight for the fuel dock, which seemed like a reasonable enough plan for a short day.

It stopped being simple almost immediately.

Somebody had spent the night on the fuel dock, and a center console that had apparently been towed in overnight was tied up dead in the middle of the face dock, which left just enough room to make things annoying without quite making them impossible.

Between the wind and current, getting in there and tied up was one of those little jobs that feels more ridiculous than difficult. Manageable, just needlessly stupid.

Once we were secured, I authorized the self-service pump, pulled the handle, and got absolutely nothing.

That turned out not to be a mechanical failure, just a small masterpiece of bad setup. The pump handles had been switched around, so the hose I was holding was not the one tied to the pump I had just paid to activate.

The dock attendant showed up at 9:00, we sorted that part out, and by 9:45 we were finally done taking fuel. So much for the quick getaway.

We took on 520 gallons by the time it was over.

At 10:19 we finally got underway for the run north.

Luckily it was a short enough leg that the late start did not create much drama. It was 48 degrees leaving out, with 20 knots of southerly wind, and once we got moving the weather turned rougher for a while than the morning had suggested. We had 25-knot southerlies, building swells, and a few rain squalls along the way.

None of it lasted all day, though. By the time we were getting in, things had cleaned back up enough to make arrival feel civilized again.

We reached Channel Club Marina in Monmouth Beach at 3:30 this afternoon, topped the fuel back off, pumped out the black water tank, and settled the boat into her summer-home slip.

After the Atlantic City fuel dock performance earlier, a normal landing and a quiet finish felt like a pretty fair reward.

All in all, it was a later start than planned, but still a short final push and a good landing. One of the stranger deliveries I have done, if only because even the easy parts kept trying to be difficult.

Now there is one more night aboard, because the flight home got overbooked, followed by an early-morning Uber and whatever the next adventure turns out to be.
