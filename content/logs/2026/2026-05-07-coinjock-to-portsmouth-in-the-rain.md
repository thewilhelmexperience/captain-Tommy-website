We left Coinjock at 7:30 this morning after waiting a little while for the tide to flood.

As it turned out, we probably could have left earlier. We carried about 10 feet of water under the boat through the first skinny section, which is always a nice confirmation after you have already spent the extra time waiting.

From there it turned into one of those days that was mostly uneventful, but not especially comfortable.

It rained the entire run.

Not a full downpour, just the steady kind of rain that limits visibility all day and never gives you a real break from it. Running through the Great Dismal Swamp in that kind of weather actually felt pretty appropriate. If there is a place that can carry a gray, wet mood without seeming offended by it, that is probably it.

The temperature kept dropping as the day went on, which made defogging the windshield a constant battle. We kept the hairdryer close by and put it to work often enough that it basically earned a crew position.

While we were waiting at Great Bridge, we heard something rattle. It turned out to be an alignment screw for the center windshield wiper. The wiper kept working, but I was immediately more interested in the second screw than the first one. If that other one had gone missing too, I would have been mostly blind at the helm in a cold rain, which is not an arrangement I recommend. It held together long enough to get us in, and I will fix it tonight.

The other annoyance was timing. Both of the automated railroad bridges dropped right as we got to them, so between the two of them we probably burned about 45 minutes waiting on trains to pass and the bridges to open back up.

None of it was dramatic. Just one of those travel days where the work is less about heroics and more about staying patient, staying dry enough, and keeping the view out the front of the boat functional.

Given the small craft advisory shaping up offshore later today, Portsmouth made a lot more sense than trying to force the next leg. We tied up at Tidewater Yacht Marina this evening and called it there.

It is 55 degrees, raining, and pretty uncomfortable outside, but the boat is warm, so no real complaints. Now we wait and hope the ocean lays down enough to make the final push to New Jersey in decent conditions.
