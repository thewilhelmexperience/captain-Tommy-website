We were set up for a 6:15 departure out of Beaufort this morning to catch the sunrise and get an early start on the run to Coinjock.

Right as we were ready to drop lines, the port engine stalled.

I checked the primary fuel filter and found it a little fouled, but not especially bad. Still, there was no point in arguing with the engine. I changed the filters, re-primed the motor, and let it run for about half an hour before trusting it enough to leave the dock.

So instead of a clean sunrise departure, we finally got underway at 7:25.

Once we were moving, the morning looked a lot better. It was 70 degrees, with light south winds and a rising tide, which is a nice enough way to point the boat north after an irritating delay at the dock.

That is how these mornings go sometimes. The plan was simple, the boat had one small objection, and the job became handling it before it had a chance to become a bigger one.

By 1:00 this afternoon we were running smoothly on the Pungo River. The wind had built to 20 knots, but it was directly behind us, so it felt more like a favor than a problem. Sunny, 80 degrees, and finally one of those easy stretches where the boat just settles in and does her job.

After being stuck waiting on repairs for a week, that felt especially good.

Sometimes the best part of the day is simply being back in motion.

By 4:15 we were a few miles south of the Alligator River Bridge and still running smoothly, which is exactly the kind of uneventful progress you appreciate after losing a week to repairs.

We landed in Coinjock at 7:00, and it turned into a good day after all.
