We were firing up the motors in Charleston at 5:36 this morning and getting ready to pull off the dock for Beaufort, North Carolina.

By 6:00 we were leaving the harbor in that early grey light, with one eye on the weather and the boat already settling into the day’s run.

Once properly underway, the next decision point came into focus. We were showing a 1:06 p.m. ETA to Frying Pan, and that was going to be the place where we checked the weather again and decided whether we were going to get forced inside from Cape Fear up to Beaufort.

The hope was that the forecast would slip a little and hold the stronger winds off until later in the afternoon. If it did, we could stay out on the ocean and make Beaufort at a reasonable hour instead of turning the day into an inside-route salvage job.

By early afternoon that question answered itself. We made the call to divert into Morningstar in Southport, North Carolina after a squall came through and brought 50-knot gusts, along with a free freshwater rinse-down whether we wanted one or not.

On the other side of it, the sea state had turned into the kind of mess that makes the decision for you: three feet of southbound chop stacked against three feet of northbound swell. Not horrible yet, but clearly deteriorating, and there was no point in pressing on toward Wrightsville Beach Inlet just to prove we could still be uncomfortable for longer.

In the end we landed safely at Morningstar Marina in Southport. We topped off the fuel, rinsed the boat down, and then used the extra time earned by stopping earlier than normal to catch up on engine-room housekeeping and get ready for tomorrow’s long push to Coinjock.
