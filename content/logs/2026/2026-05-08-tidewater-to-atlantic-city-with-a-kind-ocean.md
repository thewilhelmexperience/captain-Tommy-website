We came off the dock at Tidewater at 6:45 this morning in 45-degree air with no wind at all, which felt like a pretty fair trade after yesterday’s cold rain.

The plan was simple enough: head north, get outside, and let the ocean decide whether the day ended in Ocean City or Atlantic City.

Once we were clearing the Chesapeake, we passed a few battleships and a submarine on the way out, which is a decent way to start any travel day.

Outside, the forecast turned out to be telling the truth for once.

The ocean was friendly from the beginning. Sunny, smooth, and running with about 2-foot long-interval swells early on. Then it calmed down even more. For a while it went nearly full flat, the kind of stretch where the boat just settles in and quietly eats up miles.

By then we had made the reservation in Atlantic City, which was a good sign that the day had stopped being a question and started becoming a plan.

The only real thing to watch for through the middle of the run was crab pots, but there were not many of them, and after a point we stopped seeing them altogether.

Passing Ocean City, the sea was still easy, with just a small following swell starting to build as the wind came up a little behind us. Nothing you could really feel yet, just a reminder that the ocean was still out there and not entirely decorative.

Closer to Absecon Inlet, about 6 miles off, that following swell had built into 3- to 4-foot seas. Still manageable, still a good ride, just no longer the glassy middle stretch we had been enjoying.

We got into Farley Marina and were docked and buttoned up by 6:45 this evening.

The only part that required any extra care was the final docking. There were about 20 knots of wind blowing across the marina, which is enough to keep things honest when you are tired and almost done.

Still, all in all, it was a great day’s run. Hard to complain about cold morning air, clear skies, a kind ocean, and making Atlantic City in one shot to set up an easier finish tomorrow.
