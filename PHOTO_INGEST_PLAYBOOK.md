# Photo Ingest Playbook

Project: `captain-tommy-website`
Date: 2026-04-15

## Purpose

When the external-drive photo gallery arrives, do not immediately start dropping images into the site at random.

First, convert the gallery into a curated narrative asset pool.

This playbook exists so future sessions can process the image dump cleanly and consistently.

## Primary objective

Extract the gold from the gallery and turn it into:
- a coherent visual story
- strong homepage image placements
- candidate story clusters
- potential route-map / location-based travel narrative assets

## Overall rule

Do not treat the gallery like a normal photo dump.
Treat it like source material for a story world.

## First-pass workflow

1. Locate the full gallery source on the external drive.
2. Inventory the file tree and identify:
   - main photo folders
   - likely exports
   - duplicates
   - screenshots / junk
   - memes / jokes / unrelated images
3. Read metadata where available:
   - EXIF timestamp
   - GPS/location
   - camera/device
   - resolution / orientation
4. Split into rough buckets:
   - strong candidates
   - maybe useful
   - duplicates
   - junk / irrelevant
   - funny but maybe usable memes
5. Build a curated shortlist for website use.

## Selection priorities

Prioritize photos that signal:
- place
- motion
- atmosphere
- real life underway
- competence
- earned beauty
- a memorable human point of view

Best likely categories:
- helm shots
- underway / passage shots
- East Coast atmosphere
- Bahamas water and anchorages
- Great Lakes horizon / delivery energy
- Fort Lauderdale / home-water identity shots
- subtle lifestyle or dockside detail shots
- vessel context shots

## Avoid / deprioritize

Avoid overloading the site with photos that feel:
- generic
- staged
- touristy in a shallow way
- repetitive marina filler
- low-quality screenshots
- cluttered phone-camera junk
- visually cool but reputation-damaging

Especially avoid anything that could imply:
- recklessness
- intoxication
- carelessness
- irresponsible operation
- try-hard posturing

## Memes rule

Memes are not automatically trash.

If a meme or joke image genuinely fits Tommy’s voice and the site’s tone, keep it in a separate "maybe-funny" bucket for possible future use.

But do not let meme energy dilute the core identity of the site.
The site is a credible, human adventure archive first.

## EXIF / map logic

If GPS data exists and is usable:
- cluster images by region
- identify repeated routes / places
- build candidate stop groupings
- support route-map storytelling with real geography

If GPS data is absent or partial:
- use time clustering
- infer chapters by folder context and image content
- never fake precision

## Website target buckets

During curation, maintain at least these candidate buckets:
- `hero-candidates`
- `voyages-waters`
- `route-map-stops`
- `polaroid-stack`
- `captains-log-support`
- `boat-chapters`
- `contact/about-portrait`
- `hold-for-later`

## Story clustering questions

When grouping strong photos, ask:
- What chapter of Tommy’s life does this belong to?
- What place does this evoke?
- Does this photo feel like movement, place, boat, or personal presence?
- Could this support a short log entry or story card?
- Does this make Tommy look capable and human?

## Narrative outcome

The goal is not "best photos" in the abstract.
The goal is:
- best story-leading photos
- best place-defining photos
- best identity-defining photos
- best atmosphere-building photos

## Deliverables to create after ingest

Once the gallery is processed, create:
1. a photo inventory manifest
2. a shortlist manifest
3. a grouped narrative manifest by chapter/place
4. a note on EXIF/GPS coverage quality
5. a list of best immediate website placements

## Final quality test

Before promoting any photo to a featured site position, ask:
- Does this feel real?
- Does this feel earned?
- Does this help tell the kind of story this site wants to tell?
- Does this make Tommy look interesting without making him look reckless?
- Would this still feel right if a friend, family member, or employer saw it?

If not, do not feature it.
